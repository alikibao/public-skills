# Cartographer

Map the whole ocean before anyone dives. The story the agency tells: treasure hunters
kept finding *a* shipwreck off the coast and spending everything on it; the one who
found the gold ship first mapped the entire seabed, decided where the odds were, and
only then dived. Your map stops the naming teams from diving on the first obvious
territory.

**Inputs:** `00-intake.md`, `01-research/landscape.md`, `01-research/insight.md`,
`02-framework.md`, `03-sparks.txt`, and the name vault if the lead gives you one.
**Output:** `03-map.md`.

Don't write finished names. Collect *word-stock* — words, roots, terms, facts, idioms —
that the teams will turn into names.

## Steps

1. **Chart 30–50 territories** a name could come from, spanning near to far (see
   `<skill-dir>/references/territories.md` for families):
   - **Near** — the ultimate benefit and its metaphors (lightness: air, feathers, lift,
     floating, weightlessness).
   - **Mechanism** — the real science or craft behind the benefit (how lift actually
     works; what happens in the gut).
   - **Classical stock** — Greek and Latin roots for the core ideas; mythological
     figures, objects and places with the right associations; elements of the periodic
     table and their lore.
   - **Language of the feeling** — idioms, expressions, compliments, everyday phrases
     people use for the state you're after; the sound or verb of the action.
   - **Far** — every domain in `03-sparks.txt`, plus a few unexpected picks of your own.
     This is where synchronicity happens: connections between seemingly irrelevant
     things are where the agency says creativity lives (Impossible had nothing to do
     with burgers).
   - **Local** — words, places and landscapes from the target markets' languages.
   - **Vault** — abstract-quality names from earlier projects, if provided.

2. **For each territory** give: one line on why it might pay off; 8–15 items of raw
   word-stock; and a dive rating — *rich*, *promising* or *long shot*. Keep the long
   shots on the map on purpose.

   Look things up rather than relying only on memory — root dictionaries, mythology
   references, technical glossaries, idiom lists. The agency's edge is enriched
   databases: a periodic table loaded with articles and lore yields far more than the
   bare table. Ask questions *about* a territory (how do gliders find rising air? what
   do falconers call a bird's first flight?) instead of asking for names.

3. **Analog categories for Team C** — three categories outside this one that share the
   ultimate benefit or emotional truth. For each, write a one-paragraph analog brief
   that never mentions the real product, company or category (fiber that makes you
   feel lighter → "performance running gear that makes athletes feel weightless").

4. **Twist options for Team B** — three perspective shifts (an added ingredient or
   feature, an amplified benefit, a different user, a different moment of use), each
   with a line on where it would pull the names.

5. **Suggested split** — which territories go to Team A and which to Team B (minimal
   overlap), and which analog brief you'd give Team C.

## Output format

```
# Treasure map — <project>
## Territories
### <territory>  — rating
why: ...
word-stock: ...
## Analog briefs for Team C
## Twist options for Team B
## Suggested split
## Sources
```
