# Where names come from: territories, types and moves

## Territory families

Each territory yields **word-stock** (roots, terms, facts, idioms), not finished names.
The Cartographer charts 30–50 territories across these families; the teams dig into
their share.

1. **The ultimate benefit and its metaphors** — lightness → air, feathers, lift,
   floating, sky, bubbles.
2. **Mechanism and science** — the physics, biology, chemistry or craft behind the
   benefit (aerodynamics for lift; fermentation for gut health).
3. **Classical roots** — Greek and Latin words for the core ideas; combine, truncate,
   re-suffix. Pentium drew on *pent-* (five) and the feel of an element name.
4. **Mythology and legend** — Greek, Roman, Norse, Egyptian, African, Asian and other
   traditions: figures, objects, places. Check the full story; some carry baggage.
5. **The periodic table and its lore** — elements, discoverers, Latin names, properties.
6. **Nature** — animals, plants, fruit, weather, sky, sea, geology (BlackBerry, Azure,
   Toro).
7. **The language of the feeling** — idioms, compliments, everyday expressions for the
   state you want people in ("I feel light").
8. **The sound or verb of the action** — onomatopoeia, motion verbs (Swiffer).
9. **Claims and attitudes** — words that make a promise or take a stance (Impossible).
10. **Crafts and trades** — tools, techniques and jargon of skilled work.
11. **Places and local language** — landscapes, local words, landmarks of the target
    markets. (Pure place names can be hard to protect.)
12. **Exploration, navigation, astronomy** — instruments, maps, stars, routes.
13. **Music, art, architecture, design** — terms, instruments, movements.
14. **Far domains (sparks)** — fields chosen at random with `name_tools.py sparks`;
    the home of synchronicity.
15. **Analog categories** — other industries that share the ultimate benefit (Team C).
16. **The vault** — names from past projects filed by abstract quality (small, fast,
    light, reliable, warm, bold…).

## Name types (from most to least descriptive)

| Type | Examples | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| Descriptive | Fiber One, Ready Mop | Instantly clear; usually invisible and hard to protect |
| Suggestive | Benefiber | Hints at the benefit; often crowded |
| Real word, new context | Feather, BlackBerry, Azure, Impossible, Windsurf | Fluent *and* surprising; often the sweet spot; watch search crowding |
| Compound | DreamWorks, HubSpot, SpaceX | Memorable rhythm; can feel generic if both halves are |
| Blend / portmanteau | (two words fused) | Can be clever; can be unpronounceable |
| Coined from roots or sound | Pentium, Febreze, Sonos, Lexus | Most ownable; needs to feel familiar or it becomes noise |
| Initials / acronym | — | Rarely right for a new brand; little to grasp |
| Founder or person name | — | Personal; transfers poorly if the founder leaves |

## Moves for turning word-stock into names

- Put a real word into a new context.
- Collide two words (compound) or fuse them (blend).
- Truncate, extend or re-suffix a word.
- Build from roots; coin a word that sounds like it means something.
- Play with sound: CV rhythm, repetition, plosives, a deliberate X or Z.
- Name the sound or the verb of the action.
- Make a claim.
- Borrow from another language (check meaning in every market).
- Respell — sparingly; each respelling costs spelling-from-hearing.
- Pair a bold name with a descriptor that carries the category.
