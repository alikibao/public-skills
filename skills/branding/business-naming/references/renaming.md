# Should we rename? And how to launch one

## Principles

- **Names compound.** The longer a name is in market, the more familiarity it banks —
  which is why the cost of fixing a wrong name rises every month.
- **Bad names create friction every day**: misspellings, failed searches, confusion
  with others, the need to explain. That cost is quiet but permanent.
- **Fear of losing momentum is usually overstated.** The agency says it has never seen
  evidence of a rename losing equity or momentum *when the launch is done with
  enthusiasm and a clear story*, and customers understand they're not losing the
  product they rely on.
- **Stage matters.** For a young company (roughly seed to Series B), a rename is a
  focused 6–9 month effort, not a crisis. For a giant, long-established brand, it needs
  a compelling reason.
- **Legal threat makes it simple.** If you're being sued or can't register the mark,
  the decision is made for you.
- **Commitment matters.** A rename forced on owners who privately want to keep the name
  tends to fail. If the founders aren't open to it, explore the case with them first
  rather than starting a project.

## Assessment (do this before any naming work)

1. **Score the current name** on `evaluation.md`, honestly, and place it in a zone.
2. **Gather friction evidence:** misspellings in search queries and support tickets,
   typed-URL errors, confusion with other companies, how often the team has to explain
   it, how findable it is in search, trademark or legal exposure, and whether it fits
   where the company is going (new products, new markets).
3. **Estimate equity:** how much recognition exists (customers, search volume, press,
   partners) and how much of it is tied to the name versus the product.
4. **Stage and cost:** company stage, number of touchpoints (domains, apps, contracts,
   signage, packaging), and who has to be told.
5. **Recommend one of three:**
   - **Rename** — friction is real and growing, equity is modest or portable.
   - **Keep and support** — the name is okay but under-explained; add a descriptor or
     positioning line that carries the category.
   - **Keep** — the name works; the problem is elsewhere.

Put the recommendation to the user with the evidence, and let them decide.

## The launch story (if renaming)

A rename is a story, not an announcement. Structure:

1. **Where we were** — what the old name stood for, with respect.
2. **Why we're changing** — where the company is going.
3. **What stays the same** — the product, the people, the data, the commitments.
4. **What's new for you** — the concrete benefits (A, B, C).
5. **The new name and what it says.**

Transition checklist: secure domains and handles first; redirects from every old URL;
app-store listings; legal entity and contracts; payment descriptors; email domains;
social profiles; a "formerly <old name>" period of 6–12 months in search snippets,
signatures and descriptions; an internal launch before the external one.
